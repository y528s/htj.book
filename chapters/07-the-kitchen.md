## Questions this room raises

**Is kosher food blessed by a rabbi?** No. Kosher is a system: rules about ingredients, about keeping things apart, and someone checking it happens. The rabbi is the auditor, not a wizard.

**What makes meat kosher?** The animal has to be on the permitted list: cud-chewing with split hooves, or a named list of birds. A trained specialist slaughters it quickly by hand, it is inspected, and the blood is drawn out with salt. That is why kosher meat costs more, and why "kosher-style" pastrami isn't kosher.

**Are all fish kosher?** No. Fins and scales. Salmon yes, prawns never. The reason is that the Torah says so, not health.

**Why no milk in a meat kitchen?** Meat and milk are kept completely apart. The cleanest way to guarantee that in a meat kitchen is no dairy at all.

**Why won't my friend have a cappuccino after a burger?** After meat, observant people wait before dairy. How long depends on where their family is from. Some will say "I'm still meaty." Let them have it.

**Why baby spinach and not full-size?** Bugs. Eating one small insect is said to break more rules than eating bacon. Baby leaves are easier to check. That is also why you may see a chef inspecting a raspberry on a light box as if it has done something wrong.

**Is vegan food automatically kosher?** No. Kosher is also about how it was made and what it was made on. A latke fried in a pan that had bacon in it that morning isn't kosher.

**Who is the mashgiach?** The kosher supervisor. On one question only, is this kosher, they outrank the chef.

**Is wine holy?** No wine is holy, but wine runs right through Jewish life, from Friday kiddush to the wedding canopy. Mevushal wine has been heat-treated, so anyone can pour it. [Your KosherWine knowledge belongs here.]

**I thought Jews didn't drink?** Different faith. There is a habit of drinking at celebrations, and one day a year, Purim, where slightly overdoing it is close to the point.

**What are they washing before bread?** Their hands, ritually, with a two-handled cup. Then the table goes silent until the bread is blessed. Nothing is wrong. Let them get to the amen.

**Why is the table singing after dessert?** Grace after meals. The booklets have God's name in them, so they never go in the bin.
