# Chapter 10: The Gates (v0.1)

You notice the security at a Jewish building before you are properly through the door. The gates. The second set of gates. Someone asking who you are before they let you in. It is fair to wonder about it.

Security is a funny thing, and it's a lot more serious than security theatre that exists purely as a deterrent. In recent years alone there has been the burning of Jewish medical vehicles in London (it's worth noting that the organisation, Hatzola, serves Jewish and non-Jewish people when they call for help, and often arrives much faster than 999 can, thanks to its very high density of volunteers), and the Heaton Park attack on Yom Kippur.

Unfortunately, terrorism is real, and unlike the IRA, who would often politely call ahead with a warning, which saved lives in Manchester in 1996, such etiquette no longer exists. Not so at the Grand Hotel in Brighton in 1984. I spent my entire life being told it blew up the day I was born. I was less than twenty-four hours old when it happened, but it happened in my home town. Given that Jewish mothers have a flair for exaggeration, and the emotions that flow after having your first child could justify a dramatic memory, it doesn't take a professional to suggest the story of this event became some sort of core implanted memory in my psyche, and could have influenced how I feel in public spaces.

Some synagogues and Jewish buildings require all guests to register their visit ahead of time, so background checks can be performed in advance of their arrival. Sadly, Dara Horn's book People Love Dead Jews isn't just a catchy title. It's a reality we face every day.

I grew up with this. In 1996, my house shook one Shabbat morning as the Arndale in Manchester was blown apart. In October 2025, on Yom Kippur, the holiest day of the year, a synagogue in the same city was attacked and people at prayer were murdered.

That last one reached me in Kansas City. The synagogue was my rabbi's family shul, the place his father goes, always early for the service. I was the one who had to tell him what had happened, on the holiest morning of the year. There is no way to make that phone call gently.

I do not tell you this to frighten you. I tell you so the gates are not mistaken for drama. They are not suspicion. They are care. They exist so a parent can drop a child at nursery without a knot in their stomach, and so an eighty-year-old can come to pray without watching the door.

Having been closer to terror and antisemitism in England than I ever was living in Israel, here is what I have come to believe. This hatred is aimed at all of us, and its effect is the opposite of what it intends. We are one global family, dysfunctional at times, but that is rather the point of family. What is meant to drive us apart tends to pull us closer.

## Questions this room raises

**What is the CST?** The Community Security Trust, a charity set up in 1994 that protects the UK Jewish community and trains volunteers. Its presence is a good sign, not an alarming one.

No, we don't have horns, nor do we have sex through a hole in a sheet. The only time we control the media is when we hire a highly qualified PR firm, the same as everyone else with something to promote.

**Do Jews have horns?** No, and the origin is genuinely interesting. When Moses came down the mountain, the Hebrew says his face was "karan", radiant. The word is close to the word for horns, and the Latin translator went with horned. Centuries of art followed, Michelangelo's Moses included. A mistranslation that turned into a slander.

**Are Jews cheap, or obsessed with money?** For centuries in Europe, Jews were barred from owning land and from most trades. Lending money was one of the few jobs left open, and one Christians were forbidden to do. A whole people were forced into a job, resented for it, then smeared for being good at it. It is not a trait. It is a trap built by the people who then complained about it.

**Do Jews secretly control the banks or the media?** No. This is the same lie in different hats. There is no secret hand, only the long habit of blaming a hidden group for a complicated world. If you remember one thing from this chapter, make it this.

**Then why so many Jews in Hollywood, medicine or law?** The same history the other way up. Locked out of land, families leaned on what couldn't be confiscated: education, skills, portable professions. Hollywood was a new industry the establishment thought vulgar, so nobody minded outsiders taking it on. That is not a takeover. It is what people do with the doors left open.

**A Jewish person was rude to me. Is that a Jewish thing?** No. Some people are rude. That is people, not Jewishness.
